# EII - Remote Sensing

**Document version:** 0.1.0 · 21 October 2026  
**Classification reference:** EII Domain Taxonomy v1.0.0

An extraction of the EII Reference Register showing indicators that satellites or drones can help obtain. It retains the original 15 domains and stable indicator references. Some indicators are mapped from imagery, others are calibrated estimates, and some use remote observations as one input alongside field measurements or models.

## Reading the tables

**Mapped/observed quantities** include visible cover, geometry, surface change and instrument signals. **Retrieved/calibrated estimates** include surface temperature, moisture, leaf area and biomass. **Combined/model estimates** need additional weather, field, biological or accounting data. These routes are identified in the measurement column; satellite and drone capabilities are not interchangeable.

Only the remotely supported component of a grouped indicator is included. A satellite atmospheric column differs from a ground sensor reading; water-surface temperature differs from a depth profile; soil-surface estimates differ from root-zone or whole-profile measurements. A colour or greenness change is not automatically a diagnosis of health, species identity or ecosystem function.

Supporting indicators remain useful even when they cannot be remotely measured. Links to indicators included here stay within this file; other links open the full reference register. RS source codes refer to the existing GISDS catalogue and are candidate routes, not exact datasets already used. Field-only methods, ordinary GIS overlays and future climate projections are not included merely because they use a map.

## D05 — Soil biology

No soil-biological indicator in the current register is selected as a satellite/drone measurement. Microbial diversity, genes, biomass and activity require samples or biological assays. Remote imagery can help choose sampling locations and provide environmental context; it does not supply those biological measurements.

|---|---|---|
| RGB drone camera | Visible cover, object counts and photogrammetric surface geometry | Cannot supply NIR, red-edge, SWIR, thermal or fluorescence bands unless an appropriate additional sensor is fitted. |
| Multispectral optical satellite/drone | Vegetation indices and calibrated canopy properties | Check exact wavelengths; many drone cameras lack SWIR, so NDMI/NBR are not generally available from those cameras. |
| Thermal satellite/drone | Surface/canopy/water temperatures and inputs to water-use models | Surface temperature is not air temperature; stress and evapotranspiration require reference/weather information. |
| LiDAR satellite/drone | Sampled ground/canopy heights and vegetation layers | Coverage, occlusion and ground classification matter; satellite LiDAR is not automatically a continuous individual-tree map. |
| Photogrammetric drone or suitable satellite stereo | Visible 3D geometry and repeat surface change | Dense canopy can hide the ground and lower vegetation layers. |
| Radar/microwave satellite | Inundation, surface moisture and some structural information | Backscatter is affected by moisture, vegetation, roughness and geometry; estimates need specific retrieval algorithms. |
| Specialised atmospheric or hyperspectral satellite instruments | Atmospheric columns, aerosols, detailed spectra and some fluorescence products | These capabilities are not supplied by an ordinary satellite image or consumer drone camera. |

Soil biology and interaction domains are deliberately retained without remotely measured entries. Laboratory pH, available nutrients, microbial genes, pollination rates, groundwater recharge, full carbon sequestration and ocean carbonate chemistry should not be presented as direct satellite/drone measurements. Some can be modelled with remote covariates, but such broad correlation alone is not enough for inclusion here.
